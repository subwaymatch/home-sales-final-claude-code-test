How the numbers are computed

  • Valid price means sale_price from $10,000 to $5,000,000, inclusive. Every question uses valid prices, except Q2 and Q3 (all rows), and Q9 (all rows with a sale date).
  • City means city and state together, such as "Champaign, IL". Campus area means the university column.
  • Days on market is contract_date minus list_date, in days, using only sales that have both dates. A blank date is not zero.
  • Year and month come from sale_date.
  • Median is used unless a question asks for a mean. When two or more cities or campus areas tie, every tied one is named.
  • Rounding: dollars to whole dollars; days, miles and percents to one decimal place.
  • Repeated street addresses are kept. listing_id is the key, and parcel_number tells units in one building apart.

Data and methods

Source

This page uses the College-town home sales dataset, version 1.0, in data/college_town_home_sales.csv. Each row is one completed home sale, with the dates the home was listed, went under contract and closed. The file is read and used as it is. Nothing is removed from it, and no value is changed. The values are as reported by the collection source, not checked against county records, so an address, parcel number or assessed value may differ from the county's own file.

Rules used

  • Price rule. A sale has a valid price when sale_price is from $10,000 to $5,000,000, inclusive. Most figures use valid-price sales only. The exceptions are Q2 and Q3, which use all rows, and Q9, which uses all rows with a sale date. Each question says which rule it uses.
  • Days on market is contract_date minus list_date, in days, using only sales that have both dates. Sales where either date is blank are left out of those figures. A blank date is not treated as zero. sale_date minus list_date is not used, because it adds the time to close.
  • Missing values. A blank sale date means the sale is left out of the year and month figures (Q4 and Q9 and the year chart in Explore). A blank tax amount is left out of Q6. A blank distance is left out of distance medians. In Explore, a blank garage, basement, fireplace, living-area or year-built value is treated as unknown, not as "no" or zero. A sale with an unknown value is left out only when a filter uses that field, and the page shows how many were left out. A living area of 0 is treated as unknown.
  • Campus area is the university column, which has 11 values. City is the city and state together, such as "Champaign, IL", which has 18 values.
  • Year and month come from sale_date. Data for 2026 covers only part of the year, because the data was collected in March 2026.
  • Medians are used unless a question asks for a mean. A median of an even number of sales is the average of the two middle values. Q2 is the one question that uses a mean.
  • Ties. When two or more cities or campus areas tie, every tied place is named.
  • Rounding. Dollars are rounded to whole dollars. Days, miles and percents are rounded to one decimal place, with halves rounded up.
  • Street addresses are not unique. Sales that share an address are kept. listing_id identifies each row, and parcel_number tells units in one building apart.
  • Map positions. Each city on the map is placed at the median latitude and longitude of its sales. These are not campus locations, which the dataset does not give.
  • Distance (miles_to_campus) is straight-line distance, not driving distance.

Known limits

These are limits of the data itself, from its description sheet. The page does not correct them.

  • Some sale prices are implausible, such as a sale of $0 or one above $5,000,000. The valid-price rule removes them from the figures that use it.
  • home_type is unreliable, so the page does not use it. Some living areas are 0, which the page treats as unknown. Some tax amounts are implausibly large, and Q6 uses only taxes that are above 0 and below the sale price. Lot size is not used.
  • Days on market can be measured only for sales with both dates, and those sales are not a random sample. Homes sold without a listing are often new construction.
  • tax_assessed_value is not the sale price, so the page does not compare the two.

Checking the numbers

The page computes Q1 to Q6 in the browser. scripts/check_q1_q6.py computes the same six answers a second way, with pandas, from the same CSV file. Run it from the repository root with python3 scripts/check_q1_q6.py. The script prints its results and does not write any file.
